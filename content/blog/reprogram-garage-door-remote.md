Reprogramming a garage door remote is one of the few garage door jobs most homeowners can do safely themselves. The steps vary by brand and model, so always follow your opener's manual. This guide explains the general process and what to do when a remote will not pair.

## Before you start

- Find the opener's **brand and model number**. It is usually on a label on the motor unit.
- Look for the **"Learn" button** on the motor unit, often under the light cover or near the antenna wire. Its colour often tells you which remotes are compatible.
- Have a **sturdy step ladder** and a fresh battery for the remote.
- Make sure the door is **closed** and nobody is in its path while you test.

## General steps to pair a remote

The exact sequence differs between brands, but most openers with a Learn button work like this:

1. Press and release the **Learn** button on the motor unit. A light will usually turn on or blink.
2. Within the time window given in the manual (often about 30 seconds), press and hold the **remote button** you want to use.
3. Release it when the opener light blinks or you hear a click.
4. Press the remote button again to test. The door should move.

Older openers use **DIP switches**: small switches inside the remote and the motor unit that must be set to the same pattern. Openers built before the late 1990s may lack modern safety features, so it is worth having one that old checked.

## Programming a keypad

Wireless keypads follow a similar Learn process. You usually press Learn, then enter a new PIN and confirm it. Choose a PIN that is not easy to guess, and change it if you think someone else knows it.

## Clearing lost or stolen remotes

If a remote is lost or stolen, or you have just moved into a home, **erase all remotes** and re-pair only the ones you have. On most openers you hold the Learn button down until the light goes out. This removes every paired remote and keypad. Check your manual first.

## Car-integrated openers

Many cars have built-in garage door buttons. Pairing them usually needs a working handheld remote, plus the Learn button for newer rolling-code openers. Your car manual explains the steps.

## When a remote will not pair

- **Weak battery:** replace it first
- **Incompatible remote:** not every remote works with every opener or Learn-button colour
- **Range problems:** LED bulbs and other electronics can interfere with the signal
- **Lock or vacation mode** switched on at the wall control
- **Faulty receiver or logic board** in the opener

If the remote works but the door will not close, the problem is likely the safety sensors. See [why a garage door won't close](/blog/garage-door-wont-close-common-causes/).
