An exterior keypad lets you open the garage door without a remote. When it stops working, the cause is often simple: a dead battery, a locked wall control or a keypad that has lost its pairing with the opener. Here are the checks to try, in order.

## 1. Does the opener work at all?

Press the wall button inside the garage. If the door does not move, the problem is the opener or the door, not the keypad. See [garage door won't open? causes and what to do](/blog/garage-door-wont-open/).

## 2. Replace the battery

Most wireless keypads run on a battery. A weak battery may light the keypad but fail to send a signal. Replace it with the type listed in the manual or on the battery cover.

## 3. Check for lock or vacation mode

Many wall controls have a lock feature that blocks keypads and remotes. If your remotes have also stopped working, look for a lock button or a flashing light on the wall control.

## 4. Re-enter the code carefully

Make sure you are using the current code and pressing the enter key at the end if your keypad needs one. If someone changed the code, it will need to be reset using the steps in the opener manual.

## 5. Re-pair the keypad with the opener

A power cut or a battery change can sometimes make a keypad lose its pairing. Pairing usually involves pressing the learn button on the opener and then entering a code on the keypad. The steps differ by brand and model, so follow the manual. See [how to reprogram a garage door remote](/blog/reprogram-garage-door-remote/) for how the learn button works.

## 6. Think about the weather

Very cold weather shortens battery life, and moisture can get into an older keypad. If the keypad works on mild days but not on freezing mornings, start with a new battery. A keypad with water damage or worn buttons may need replacing.

## 7. Check the signal range

The keypad needs to reach the opener's receiver. A new metal fixture, a weak antenna on the opener or interference can affect the signal. Check that the opener's antenna hangs down freely.

## Is it time for a new keypad or opener?

Replacement keypads are available for most opener brands, but they need to be compatible with your opener. Very old openers may not support newer keypads. In that case, a new opener with a keypad, smartphone control and battery backup may be the better choice. See [when to replace your garage door opener](/blog/when-to-replace-garage-door-opener/).
