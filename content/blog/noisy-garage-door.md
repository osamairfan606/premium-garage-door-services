A noisy garage door is more than an annoyance. The type of sound often tells you which part is worn, and catching it early can prevent a bigger repair. Here is what common garage door noises usually mean and what you can do about them.

## What different noises usually mean

| Noise | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Squeaking or squealing | Dry hinges, rollers or springs | Lubricate (see below) |
| Grinding or scraping | Worn rollers, dirty or bent tracks | Clean tracks; have rollers and tracks checked |
| Rattling | Loose bolts, hinges or opener mounting | Have the hardware tightened |
| Popping or jerky movement | Worn rollers, spring tension problems | Call a technician |
| Loud bang | Possibly a broken spring | Stop using the door and call |
| Humming with little movement | Opener straining against a heavy door | Stop using the opener and call |

## Safe things you can do yourself

### Lubricate the moving parts

Use a lubricant made for garage doors, such as a silicone- or lithium-based spray, not grease or general-purpose oil. Apply it to:

- Hinges
- Rollers with metal bearings (not nylon rollers without bearings)
- The opener rail or chain, if the manual recommends it

Do **not** lubricate the tracks themselves. Grease on the tracks collects dirt and makes rollers skid.

### Clean the tracks

Wipe the inside of each track with a dry cloth to remove dirt, leaves and grit. Check for dents, but do not try to bend tracks back into shape, because that can knock the door off balance.

### Check the opener chain

A loose chain slaps against the rail. Belt-drive openers are much quieter than chain drives. If your opener is old and loud, upgrading may be the simplest fix.

## Fixes that need a technician

- **Worn rollers.** Cracked or wobbly rollers grind and can pop out of the track. Replacing them with nylon rollers often makes the door much quieter. See [roller repair](/services/roller-repair/).
- **Loose or bent hardware.** Some brackets, especially the bottom ones, are attached to the cables and springs and must not be loosened.
- **Spring noise and imbalance.** Squealing springs can be lubricated, but a door that pops, jerks or feels heavy needs a balance check.
- **Opener problems.** Grinding gears or a straining motor are covered in [garage door opener not working](/blog/garage-door-opener-not-working/).

## Why noise matters

Noise often means parts are wearing faster than they should. A yearly [maintenance and tune-up](/services/maintenance-tune-up/) includes lubrication, hardware tightening, a balance test and a safety inspection, so small problems are fixed before they become emergencies.
