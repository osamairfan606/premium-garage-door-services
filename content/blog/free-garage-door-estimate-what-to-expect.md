A free onsite estimate lets a technician see your garage door in person before you commit to anything. It is the best way to get an accurate price, because many garage door problems look alike over the phone. Here is what happens during the visit and how to get the most out of it.

## Before the visit

Have this ready if you can:

- **What is happening:** noises, when the problem started, and whether the door opens at all
- **Photos or a short video** of the problem, if it is safe to take them
- **The opener brand and model**, usually on a label on the motor unit
- **What you want:** a repair, a new opener, or ideas for a new door

If the door is stuck, or a spring or cable looks broken, do not try to force it. See [emergency garage door repair: what to do](/blog/emergency-garage-door-repair-guide/).

## During the visit

The technician will usually:

1. Ask about the problem and look at the door, springs, cables, rollers, tracks and opener
2. Check the door's balance and safety features
3. Explain what is wrong and show you the worn or broken parts
4. Give you the options, for example repair or replacement
5. Give you a written estimate before any work begins

For a new door, the technician also measures the opening and talks through styles, insulation and openers. See [insulated garage doors: are they worth it?](/blog/insulated-garage-doors-worth-it/)

## Questions to ask

- What is the cause of the problem?
- What happens if I wait?
- Is a repair enough, or does replacement make more sense? Why?
- What is included in the price?
- What warranty comes with the parts and labour?

Our guide to [repairing or replacing your garage door](/blog/repair-or-replace-garage-door/) can help you weigh the answers.

## After the visit

You decide. A good company explains the options clearly and does not pressure you. If you approve the estimate, many repairs can be completed on the same visit when the parts are available.
