When a garage door opener stops working, the cause is often simple: a tripped outlet, a dead remote battery or a sensor that has been knocked out of line. Before you book a repair, work through these six checks. If the door itself looks damaged, crooked or heavy, stop and call a technician instead.

## 1. Check the power

Make sure the opener is plugged in and the outlet works. Plug a lamp into the same outlet to test it. If the outlet is on a GFCI circuit, press its reset button. Check the breaker panel as well. After a power cut, some openers need a minute before they respond again.

## 2. Check the lock or vacation button

Most wall controls have a **lock** or **vacation** button. It blocks every remote while still letting the wall button work. If the door opens from the wall but not from the remotes, look for a lit lock symbol and switch it off.

## 3. Replace the remote and keypad batteries

A remote with a weak battery may work only when you stand close to the door. Replace the battery first. If one remote works and another does not, the faulty one may need to be reprogrammed. The steps differ by brand and model, so follow the opener manual. If you no longer have it, our technicians can [program remotes and keypads](/services/garage-door-opener-repair-installation/) during a visit.

## 4. Look at the safety sensors

Two small sensors near the floor stop the door from closing on people, pets or cars. If the opener lifts the door but will not close it, or the light flashes, the sensors are the usual cause.

- Clear anything in the doorway.
- Wipe the lenses with a soft cloth.
- Check that the indicator lights on both sensors are steady. A blinking or dark light usually means they are out of line.

We cover this in more detail in [why a garage door won't close](/blog/garage-door-wont-close-common-causes/).

## 5. Check whether the trolley is disconnected

If someone pulled the red emergency release cord, the opener will run but the door will not move with it. With the door **fully closed**, run the opener once. On most models the trolley reconnects by itself. If you are unsure, leave it and ask a technician. Never pull the release cord while the door is open, because a door with a broken spring can drop.

## 6. Check the limit and force settings

If the door reverses just before it reaches the floor, or stops partway, the limit or force settings may need adjusting. These are set on the motor unit and are different for every brand. Small adjustments can be made by following the manual. Never raise the force setting to make a heavy door move, because that hides a spring problem and makes the safety reverse less sensitive.

## When the problem is not the opener

Openers are built to guide a balanced door, not to lift its full weight. If the motor hums or strains but the door barely moves, a [broken spring](/blog/broken-garage-door-spring-signs-and-what-to-do/) or cable is the likely cause. Running the opener against it can strip gears and burn out the motor.

## Signs your opener needs repair or replacement

- It is loud, grinds or vibrates badly
- The motor hums but the chain or belt does not move
- It reverses for no reason after the sensors check out
- It has no safety sensors, or is very old
- You want quieter operation, battery backup or smartphone control
