Midwest winters are cold and summers can be hot and humid. If your garage is attached to the house, its door is one of the largest openings in your home's outer shell. An insulated garage door can make the garage, and the rooms next to it, more comfortable. Here is how to decide whether it is worth it for you.

## What makes a garage door "insulated"

Most insulated doors are built as a sandwich: steel on the outside and inside, with insulation in between. The two common types are:

- **Polystyrene panels**, fitted inside the door sections
- **Polyurethane foam**, injected between the steel layers, where it bonds and adds stiffness

A door's insulation is usually described by its **R-value**: the higher the number, the better it resists heat flow. Manufacturers measure R-value in different ways, so compare doors from the same brand or ask how the number was calculated.

## Who benefits most

An insulated door is most worthwhile if:

- The garage is **attached** to the house
- There is a **bedroom or living space above or beside** the garage
- You use the garage as a **workshop, gym or play area**
- You keep a **second fridge or freezer**, or anything that should not freeze
- You want a **quieter, sturdier** door. Insulated doors usually rattle less and resist dents better.

It matters less for a detached garage used only for parking.

## Insulation is only half the story

A well-insulated door will not help much if cold air leaks around it. Check:

- **Bottom seal:** a worn or flattened rubber seal lets in cold air, water and snow
- **Perimeter weatherstripping** along the sides and top of the door
- **Seals between panels** on insulated sections

Replacing worn seals is often a quick win on its own. Our [winter maintenance checklist](/blog/winter-garage-door-maintenance-checklist/) covers what to look for.

## Other things to compare

- **Steel thickness (gauge):** thicker steel resists dents better
- **Style and windows:** insulated glass is available for windowed designs
- **Weight:** insulated doors are heavier, so the springs must be sized for the new door
- **Opener:** a heavier door may suit a stronger or newer opener. Read [belt vs. chain vs. wall-mount openers](/blog/belt-vs-chain-garage-door-opener/).

## Repair or replace?

If your current door is in good shape, better weatherstripping may be enough. If it is dented, drafty or near the end of its life, a new insulated door can solve several problems at once. Our guide to [repairing or replacing a garage door](/blog/repair-or-replace-garage-door/) can help you decide.
