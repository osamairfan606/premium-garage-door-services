A garage door that will not open usually has one of a handful of causes. Some are simple, such as a dead remote battery or a locked wall control. Others, such as a broken spring, need a technician. Here is how to tell the difference safely.

## First: is it the door or the opener?

Listen when you press the wall button.

- **The motor runs but the door does not move:** the problem is often a broken spring, a snapped cable or a disconnected opener.
- **The door lifts a few inches and stops, or the opener strains:** a broken spring is the most likely cause.
- **Nothing happens at all:** the opener may have no power, the wall control may be locked, or the remote may need a new battery.

## Common causes

### 1. Broken spring

Springs do the heavy lifting, not the opener. When a spring breaks, the door becomes too heavy to lift, and you may have heard a loud bang in the garage. Look for a gap in the coils of the spring above the door. **Do not try to lift the door by hand or with the opener.** See [signs of a broken garage door spring](/blog/broken-garage-door-spring-signs-and-what-to-do/).

### 2. Snapped or loose cable

The lift cables run from the bottom of the door to the springs. If one snaps, the door may hang crooked or refuse to move. See [garage door cable snapped: what to do](/blog/garage-door-cable-snapped/).

### 3. No power to the opener

Check that the opener is plugged in, that the outlet works and that a breaker or GFCI outlet has not tripped. If the opener light does not come on, power is the first suspect.

### 4. Remote or keypad problem

If the wall button works but the remote does not, replace the remote battery first. See [how to reprogram a garage door remote](/blog/reprogram-garage-door-remote/) and [garage door keypad not working](/blog/garage-door-keypad-not-working/).

### 5. Wall control locked

Many wall controls have a lock or vacation mode that blocks remotes and keypads. Check for a lock button or a flashing light on the wall control, and look in the opener manual for how to turn it off.

### 6. Opener disconnected

If someone pulled the red emergency release cord, the opener runs but is no longer connected to the door. With the door **fully closed**, the opener can usually be reconnected by running it through a cycle. Check the manual for your model.

### 7. Manual lock engaged

Some doors have a slide lock or a handle lock. If it is engaged, the opener cannot lift the door, and it can damage the door or opener.

### 8. Door off track or binding

Bent tracks, worn rollers or an object in the track can jam the door. See [garage door off track](/blog/garage-door-off-track/).

## Your car is stuck inside

If you need your car urgently and the door will not open, do not force it. Forcing a door with a broken spring or cable can make it fall. Call a technician who offers emergency service. See [emergency garage door repair: what to do](/blog/emergency-garage-door-repair-guide/).

## When to call a professional

Call for help if you see a broken spring or cable, the door is crooked, the motor runs but the door does not move, or the checks above do not solve the problem.
