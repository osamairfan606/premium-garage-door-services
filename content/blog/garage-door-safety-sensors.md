Safety sensors are the small "eyes" mounted near the bottom of your garage door tracks. They stop the door from closing on people, pets, cars and belongings. When they are dirty or out of line, the door usually refuses to close or reverses as soon as it starts moving.

## How safety sensors work

One sensor sends an invisible infrared beam across the doorway, and the other receives it. If anything breaks the beam while the door is closing, the opener stops the door and sends it back up. Openers sold in the US since the 1990s are required to have this kind of entrapment protection.

Each sensor has a small indicator light. On most models:

- **Steady lights** mean the sensors can see each other
- **A blinking or dark light** means the beam is blocked, the sensors are misaligned, or there is a wiring fault

The exact light colours and meanings vary by brand, so check the opener manual.

## Signs of a sensor problem

- The door **won't close** but opens normally
- The door **reverses** just after it starts closing
- The opener light **flashes** after you press the button
- The door closes only while you **hold down** the wall button

## Safe checks you can do

1. **Clear the doorway.** Move bins, boxes, bikes and anything else near the tracks.
2. **Clean the lenses.** Wipe them gently with a soft, dry cloth. Dust, cobwebs and condensation can block the beam.
3. **Check for sunlight.** Strong low sun shining straight into a sensor can confuse it at certain times of day.
4. **Check alignment.** Both sensors should face each other at the same height. If one has been knocked, loosen its wing nut, line it up until both lights are steady, and tighten it again.
5. **Look at the wires.** Loose, chewed or damaged wires between the sensors and the opener can cause intermittent faults.

## Test the safety reverse

With the door open, start closing it and pass an object such as a broom handle through the beam. The door should stop and reverse right away. Also test the contact reverse: place a piece of wood flat on the floor under the door. When the door touches it, it should reverse. If either test fails, stop using the opener and have it checked.

**Never bypass, remove or tape over the sensors.** They are there to prevent serious injuries, especially to children and pets.

## When to call a technician

- The lights still blink after cleaning and alignment
- The wiring is damaged
- The door still reverses with the sensors working
- The opener is old and has no sensors at all
