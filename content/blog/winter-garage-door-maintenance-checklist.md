Cold weather is hard on garage doors. Metal contracts, lubricants thicken, and springs that were already worn are more likely to break on a freezing morning. A little maintenance in the fall helps your door keep working through a Chicago or Indianapolis winter.

## Your 10-step winter checklist

### 1. Watch and listen to the door

Open and close the door a few times. Grinding, scraping, squealing or jerky movement usually means worn rollers, dry hardware or a balance problem.

### 2. Test the door's balance

Pull the emergency release cord **only with the door fully closed**, then lift the door by hand to about waist height. A balanced door stays in place. If it drops or shoots up, the springs need adjusting. Leave that work to a technician.

### 3. Inspect the springs and cables

Look for gaps in the springs, rust, or frayed cables. **Do not touch them.** If anything looks damaged, book a [spring repair](/services/broken-spring-repair/) or [cable repair](/services/cable-repair/) before the first hard freeze.

### 4. Lubricate moving parts

Use a garage-door lubricant (not grease) on hinges, rollers with metal bearings, springs and the opener rail. Cold-weather lubricant helps the door move freely when temperatures drop.

### 5. Check the rollers

Worn or cracked rollers make noise and can come out of the track. Nylon rollers are quieter and need less lubrication.

### 6. Replace worn weatherstripping

The rubber seal along the bottom of the door keeps out cold air, water and snow. If it is cracked, flattened or you can see daylight underneath, replace it. This also helps stop the door from freezing to the floor.

### 7. Clear the tracks and the floor

Remove leaves, dirt and salt from the tracks and the area under the door. Ice under the door is a common cause of reversing and strain on the opener.

### 8. Test the safety sensors and auto-reverse

Wave an object through the sensor beam while the door is closing. It should reverse right away. Then place a piece of wood on the floor under the door. When the door touches it, it should reverse.

### 9. Check the opener and remotes

Replace remote batteries and make sure the keypad works. Many older openers struggle in cold weather; if yours is slow or noisy, consider an [opener repair or upgrade](/services/garage-door-opener-repair-installation/).

### 10. Book a professional tune-up

A yearly [garage door maintenance and tune-up](/services/maintenance-tune-up/) covers lubrication, hardware tightening, balance testing and a full safety inspection. It is the easiest way to avoid a broken spring on the coldest day of the year.

## Quick winter tips

- If the door freezes to the ground, do not keep pressing the button. Gently free the seal with a plastic scraper or warm air.
- Keep the area under the door clear of snow and slush.
- Avoid slamming the door shut in very cold weather.
