Garage door springs wear out with use, not with age alone. Most are rated by the number of cycles they can handle, where one cycle is the door opening and closing once. How long that lasts in years depends on how often you use the door.

## How spring life is measured

Standard torsion springs are commonly rated for around 10,000 cycles. Higher-cycle springs are available for doors that are used often.

To estimate how long your springs might last, multiply your daily uses by 365. A door used about four times a day completes roughly 1,460 cycles a year. At that rate a 10,000-cycle spring lasts about seven years. A door used ten times a day would reach the same number of cycles in under three years.

These are estimates. Real spring life also depends on the spring's quality, the door's weight and how well the door is maintained.

## What shortens spring life

- **Heavy use.** More cycles mean a shorter life.
- **Rust.** Moisture, road salt and humid garages pit and weaken the coils.
- **An unbalanced door.** If the springs are not set correctly for the door's weight, they wear faster.
- **Cold weather.** Springs that are already worn are more likely to break in a cold snap. See [why garage door springs break in cold weather](/blog/why-garage-door-springs-break-in-cold-weather/).
- **No maintenance.** Dry springs and hardware add friction and strain.

## Signs your springs are wearing out

- The door feels heavier or the opener strains
- The door rises unevenly or jerks
- The door does not stay in place when it is part way open (checked by a technician)
- Visible gaps or stretched coils
- Rust on the springs

## Should both springs be replaced at once?

If your door has two springs, they were usually installed at the same time and have done the same number of cycles. When one breaks, the other is often close behind. Replacing both together keeps the door balanced and can save a second service visit.

## Torsion or extension springs?

Torsion springs sit on a bar above the door. Extension springs run along the horizontal tracks on each side. They wear differently and are replaced differently. See [torsion vs. extension springs](/blog/torsion-vs-extension-springs/).

## Leave spring work to a technician

Springs store enormous tension. Adjusting or replacing them without the right tools and training can cause serious injury. If you think a spring is worn or broken, stop using the door and call a professional.
