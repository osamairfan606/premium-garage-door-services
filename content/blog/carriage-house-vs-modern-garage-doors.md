A new garage door can change how your whole home looks from the street. Two of the most popular styles are carriage-style doors and modern doors. Both are available in insulated versions for Chicago and Indianapolis winters, so the choice is mostly about the look of your home and how you want the door to feel.

## Carriage-style garage doors

Carriage-style doors are designed to look like old swing-out carriage-house doors, although most open overhead like a standard sectional door.

**Typical features:**
- Panels with vertical boards or a cross-buck "X" design
- Decorative handles and hinges
- Arched or square windows across the top
- Wood, or steel and composite made to look like wood

**Suits:** Craftsman, farmhouse, colonial and traditional homes.

## Modern garage doors

Modern doors have clean lines and simple shapes.

**Typical features:**
- Flush or long horizontal panels
- Full-view glass panels in aluminium frames, or narrow window strips
- Dark colours such as black and charcoal, or wood-look finishes
- Minimal or hidden hardware

**Suits:** contemporary, mid-century and new-build homes, and updated older homes.

## Things to compare

### Materials

Steel is the most common choice: it is durable and lower maintenance. Real wood looks beautiful but needs regular finishing. Composite and wood-look steel give the wood appearance with less upkeep. Aluminium and glass suit modern designs.

### Insulation

In cold Midwest winters, an insulated door helps keep an attached garage warmer and can make the door quieter and stronger. Glass panels let in more light but insulate less than solid panels. See [insulated garage doors: are they worth it?](/blog/insulated-garage-doors-worth-it/).

### Windows and privacy

Windows brighten the garage but can show what is inside. Frosted or obscured glass gives light with privacy. See [garage door security tips](/blog/garage-door-security-tips/).

### Maintenance

Darker colours can show dust and may fade faster in strong sun. Wood needs refinishing. Steel and composite need little more than washing and an annual service.

## How to choose

- Match the style of your home's windows, front door and trim
- Look at homes on your street with a similar style
- Think about colour: the door is a large part of the front of the house
- Choose an insulated door if the garage is attached or used as a workspace
