Commercial overhead doors open and close far more often than home garage doors. Loading docks, service bays and storefront doors can cycle dozens of times a day, so springs, cables, rollers and openers wear out much faster. A simple maintenance routine reduces breakdowns, keeps staff safe and protects your premises.

## Why maintenance matters more for businesses

- **Downtime costs money.** A stuck door can stop deliveries, trap vehicles or leave a building unsecured.
- **High cycle counts.** Springs and cables are rated for a set number of cycles, and busy doors use them up quickly.
- **Safety.** Heavy commercial doors can cause serious injury if a spring, cable or safety device fails.
- **Security and energy.** A door that does not close or seal properly lets in weather and puts stock at risk.

## Daily or weekly checks staff can do

These are visual checks only. Staff should never adjust springs, cables or brackets.

- **Watch and listen** as the door opens and closes. Report grinding, banging, jerking or a door that hesitates.
- **Keep the opening clear**, including the safety sensor beams, so pallets and carts do not block them.
- **Check that the door closes fully** and seals at the bottom.
- **Look for visible damage**: dented panels, bent tracks, loose hardware or frayed cables.
- **Test the safety reverse.** The door should stop and reverse when an object breaks the sensor beam.
- **Report problems straight away** and take the door out of service if it is crooked or off track.

## What a professional service visit should cover

During a [maintenance and tune-up](/services/maintenance-tune-up/), a technician will typically:

1. Inspect springs, cables, drums and bearings for wear
2. Lubricate rollers, hinges, bearings and springs
3. Check track alignment and tighten brackets and fasteners
4. Test the door's balance and adjust spring tension if needed
5. Check the opener, limits, force settings and safety devices
6. Inspect weatherseals and bottom rubber
7. Note parts that are near the end of their life, so replacement can be planned

## How often to schedule service

The right interval depends on how often each door is used. Busy dock and service-bay doors generally need professional checks more often than doors that open a few times a day. Ask your technician to recommend a schedule for each door, based on its cycle count and condition. Keep a simple log of service dates and repairs for every door.
