A broken spring is one of the most common reasons a garage door suddenly stops working. The springs carry most of the door's weight, so when one breaks the door becomes very heavy, the opener struggles, and the door can drop without warning. If you think a spring has broken, **leave the door closed and do not try to force it open**.

## Signs your garage door spring is broken

Springs usually fail without any warning, often when the weather turns cold. Look for these signs:

- **A loud bang from the garage.** Many homeowners hear a sharp crack when a torsion spring snaps.
- **A visible gap in the spring.** A torsion spring sits on a bar above the door. If you can see a 1–2 inch gap in the coil, it has broken.
- **The door rises a few inches, then stops.** The opener's safety system senses the extra weight and reverses.
- **The door looks crooked or closes too fast.** One side may be carrying more weight than the other.
- **Loose or hanging cables.** Cables can come off their drums when spring tension is lost.
- **The opener runs but the door barely moves.** The motor is not designed to lift the full weight of the door.

## Why you should not open the door yourself

A typical double garage door is heavy, and the springs are under very high tension. That is why spring work is dangerous:

- Without spring support, the door can fall suddenly when lifted by hand.
- Winding or unwinding a torsion spring without the right tools can cause serious injury.
- Running the opener against a broken spring can damage the opener's gears or motor.

If your car is stuck inside, call for emergency service rather than lifting the door with the opener disconnected.

## Torsion vs. extension springs

Most garages in Chicago and Indianapolis use one of two systems:

| Type | Where it is | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Torsion spring | On a metal bar above the door | One or two large coiled springs |
| Extension spring | Along the horizontal tracks on each side | Long springs that stretch as the door closes |

Both types wear out with use. Each time the door opens and closes counts as one cycle, and every spring is rated for a limited number of cycles.

## What happens during a spring repair

When you book a [broken spring repair](/services/broken-spring-repair/), a technician will usually:

1. Inspect the springs, cables, drums, bearings and rollers to confirm the cause.
2. Explain the options and the price before any work starts.
3. Replace the broken spring. On doors with two springs, replacing both at once is often recommended so the door stays balanced.
4. Adjust the tension and test the balance so the door stays put when stopped halfway.
5. Check the opener's force and the safety-reverse function.

Most spring repairs are completed in a single visit.

## How to make new springs last longer

- Lubricate the springs and hinges with a garage-door lubricant a few times a year.
- Watch for a door that feels heavy or closes unevenly. These are early signs of wear.
- Book a yearly [maintenance and tune-up](/services/maintenance-tune-up/) so worn parts are caught early.
