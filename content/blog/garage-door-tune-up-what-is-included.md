A garage door tune-up is a professional maintenance visit that keeps your door running smoothly and catches problems before they turn into an emergency. Most residential doors benefit from one about once a year. Here is what a tune-up usually includes.

## What a technician checks

### Springs

The technician inspects the torsion or extension springs for rust, gaps and wear, and checks that they are balanced for the door's weight. Springs are the part most likely to fail without warning. Learn the difference in [torsion vs. extension springs](/blog/torsion-vs-extension-springs/).

### Door balance

A balanced door stays in place when it is lifted part way by hand, with the opener disconnected. An unbalanced door strains the opener and wears out parts faster.

### Cables and drums

Lift cables are checked for fraying and rust, and the drums for proper winding. See [garage door cable snapped: what to do](/blog/garage-door-cable-snapped/).

### Rollers, hinges and tracks

Worn rollers are replaced if needed, hinges are tightened and tracks are checked for alignment and dents. See [nylon vs. steel garage door rollers](/blog/nylon-vs-steel-garage-door-rollers/).

### Lubrication

Moving parts such as hinges, rollers, bearings and springs are lubricated with a garage-door lubricant.

### Hardware tightening

Daily vibration loosens bolts and brackets over time, so they are checked and tightened.

### Opener and safety features

The technician tests the opener, the safety sensors and the auto-reverse feature, and adjusts the travel limits and force settings if needed. See [garage door safety sensors explained](/blog/garage-door-safety-sensors/).

### Weatherstripping

The bottom seal and side seals are checked for cracks and gaps that let in cold air and water.

## How often should you book a tune-up?

About once a year is right for most homes. A door used many times a day, or a commercial door, benefits from more frequent checks. Many homeowners in Chicago and Indianapolis book before winter, when cold weather puts the most stress on springs and openers.

## Signs you need a tune-up now

- The door is getting noisier
- It moves unevenly or jerks
- It feels heavier than it used to
- It has been more than a year since the last service

## What you can do between visits

- Look and listen once a month while the door runs
- Keep the tracks clear of debris
- Test the auto-reverse by placing an object such as a roll of paper towels in the doorway
- Keep children away from the door and remotes

Leave springs, cables and bottom brackets to a professional. They hold dangerous tension.
