Commercial overhead doors rarely fail without warning. Most show signs first: noise, slow movement or a door that does not close properly. Spotting these early lets you plan a repair instead of dealing with a door that is stuck at the start of a busy day.

## 1. The door is noisy or grinding

Grinding, squealing or banging usually points to worn rollers, dry bearings, loose hardware or a spring problem. Noise is often the first sign that parts are wearing faster than they should.

## 2. It moves slowly or jerks

A door that hesitates, shudders or moves unevenly may have worn rollers, a track problem or an opener under strain. Uneven movement also puts extra load on the cables.

## 3. One side is higher than the other

A crooked door can mean a broken cable, a failed spring or a track problem. **Stop using it.** A door that is out of balance can come off its track or fall. Read about [doors that come off track](/blog/garage-door-off-track/).

## 4. It does not close or seal fully

Gaps at the bottom let in cold air, water and pests, and leave the building less secure. The cause may be a worn bottom seal, misaligned sensors, opener limit settings or damaged panels.

## 5. It reverses or stops unexpectedly

Blocked or misaligned safety sensors, incorrect force settings or a failing opener can all make a door reverse. Never bypass safety devices to keep a door running. See [how safety sensors work](/blog/garage-door-safety-sensors/).

## 6. Springs or cables look worn

Gaps in a torsion spring, rust, or frayed and loose cables are serious warning signs. Springs and cables are under high tension, so only a trained technician should handle them. Learn more about [snapped cables](/blog/garage-door-cable-snapped/).

## 7. The opener is struggling

If the motor hums, overheats or needs several attempts to move the door, the opener may be failing, or it may be straining against a door that is out of balance.

## 8. Visible damage

Dented panels, bent tracks, cracked sections and loose brackets, often from forklift or vehicle impacts, should be checked before they lead to a bigger failure.

## What to do

1. Take a door with serious symptoms out of service and keep people clear.
2. Note what you see and hear, and when it started.
3. Call a technician with the door type, size and opener model, if known.
