Garage door lift cables work with the springs to raise and lower the door evenly. When a cable frays, slips off its drum or snaps, the door can tilt, jam or come down hard on one side. A broken cable is a safety problem, not a do-it-yourself project.

## What garage door cables do

On a torsion system, a cable runs from each bottom corner of the door up to a drum on the spring bar. On an extension system, cables run through pulleys connected to the springs. In both cases the cables carry the lifting force from the springs to the door.

## Signs of a broken or failing cable

- The door hangs **crooked** or one side is lower than the other
- A cable is **loose, hanging or tangled** near the drum or the bottom bracket
- You see **fraying**, rust or broken strands
- The door **jerks**, slams shut or stops partway
- The opener strains, or the door will not open at all
- You heard a loud snap or bang

## Why a snapped cable is dangerous

- **The door can fall.** With one side unsupported, the door can drop or twist out of its tracks.
- **High tension.** Cables are attached to springs under heavy load. Loosening a bottom bracket or cable can release that energy suddenly.
- **More damage.** Running the door with a broken cable can bend panels, damage tracks and burn out the opener.

**Do not** try to rethread a cable, loosen the bottom brackets or adjust the drums yourself.

## What to do right now

1. Stop using the door and the opener.
2. Keep people, pets and cars away from the door.
3. Leave the door where it is. Do not try to lift it by hand.
4. Call a technician and describe what you see.

If the door is stuck open or your car is trapped, see our [emergency garage door repair guide](/blog/emergency-garage-door-repair-guide/).

## Why cables and springs often fail together

Cables and springs share the same load. A broken spring can throw a cable off its drum, and a worn cable can snap when a spring loses tension. That is why a technician checks the whole system, including springs, drums, bearings, rollers and tracks, not just the cable. Read more about [broken garage door springs](/blog/broken-garage-door-spring-signs-and-what-to-do/).

## What a cable repair involves

During a [garage door cable repair](/services/cable-repair/), a technician will usually:

1. Secure the door and release spring tension safely
2. Replace the damaged cable. Both cables are often replaced together so they wear evenly.
3. Check and, if needed, replace the drums and bottom brackets
4. Re-tension the springs and balance the door
5. Test the door's travel and the opener's safety reverse

## How to help your cables last longer

- Book a yearly [maintenance and tune-up](/services/maintenance-tune-up/)
- Have rust or fraying checked early
- Fix crooked doors and worn rollers promptly, because they put uneven strain on the cables
