Weatherstripping is the rubber and vinyl sealing around your garage door. When it wears out, cold air, rain, snow, leaves and pests get in, and the door can freeze to the ground in winter. Replacing worn seals is one of the simplest ways to make a garage more comfortable in Chicago and Indianapolis.

## Types of garage door seals

- **Bottom seal:** a rubber strip along the bottom of the door that presses against the floor. It takes the most wear.
- **Threshold seal:** a raised strip glued to the garage floor, useful where water runs toward the garage or the floor is uneven
- **Perimeter (stop) molding:** flexible seals along the sides and top of the door frame
- **Section seals:** seals between the panels of some insulated doors

## Signs your weatherstripping needs replacing

- You can see **daylight** under or around the closed door
- The bottom seal is **cracked, brittle, torn or flattened**
- **Water, snow or leaves** get into the garage
- The garage feels **drafty** or much colder than it should
- The door **freezes to the floor** in winter
- You notice **insects or rodents** getting in

## What you can safely check yourself

- Close the door and look for gaps of light from inside the garage
- Run a hand along the bottom seal and feel for cracks or hard spots
- Clean dirt and grit from the bottom seal and the floor
- Keep the area under the door clear of ice and snow

Replacing a bottom seal usually means sliding a new strip into the retainer at the bottom of the door. **Never** remove the bottom bracket or fixture, because on many doors it is attached to the lift cables and is under high spring tension. If the retainer is damaged or the door must be raised partway to do the job, leave it to a technician.

## Seals and the opener

A thicker seal or threshold can change where the door meets the floor. If the door starts reversing after new seals go on, the opener's close limit may need adjusting.
