When your garage door breaks in Chicago, especially on a cold winter morning, it is tempting to call the first number you find. A few minutes of checking can save you from overpriced repairs and unnecessary part replacements. Here is what to look for in a garage door company in Chicago.

## 1. A real local presence

Look for a company with a Chicago phone number that is answered by a person, a working website and a Google Business Profile with genuine reviews. Be careful with listings that only give a call-centre number, or that change name from one search result to the next.

## 2. Reviews that describe real jobs

Read recent reviews on Google and other sites, not just the star rating. Good reviews mention the problem, the technician and how the job went. A handful of very short, very similar five-star reviews posted at the same time is a warning sign.

## 3. A clear written estimate before work starts

A good company explains what is wrong, shows you the worn or broken part, and gives you a written estimate before starting. You should know what you are paying for, including parts and labour.

## 4. No pressure to replace parts you do not need

Some repairs genuinely need several parts. For example, it is often sensible to replace both torsion springs at once. But be cautious if a technician insists on replacing the whole door or opener without explaining why a repair will not work. Our guide to [repairing or replacing your garage door](/blog/repair-or-replace-garage-door/) can help you judge.

## 5. Warranty on parts and labour

Ask what warranty comes with the parts and the work, and get it in writing.

## 6. Insurance

Garage door work involves high-tension springs and heavy doors. Ask whether the company carries insurance in case something goes wrong at your property.

## 7. Emergency availability

Broken springs and doors stuck open do not wait for business hours. Check whether the company offers emergency service and same-day appointments. See [emergency garage door repair: what to do](/blog/emergency-garage-door-repair-guide/).

## Red flags to watch for

- A very low phone quote that rises sharply once the technician arrives
- No company name on the invoice, vehicle or uniform
- Pressure to decide on the spot
- Cash-only payment
- Refusing to show you the broken part

## Questions to ask before you book

- Is the estimate free, and is it in writing?
- What is included in the price?
- What warranty do you offer on parts and labour?
- When can a technician arrive?
