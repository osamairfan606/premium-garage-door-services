Every garage door relies on springs to lift its weight. Most homes in Chicago and Indianapolis use one of two systems: **torsion springs** or **extension springs**. Knowing which one you have helps you describe a problem when you call for help, and helps you understand the options a technician gives you.

## How to tell which springs you have

Stand inside the garage with the door closed and look up:

- **Torsion springs** sit on a metal bar directly above the door opening. You will see one or two large coiled springs wrapped around the bar, with a cable drum at each end.
- **Extension springs** run along the horizontal tracks on each side of the door, near the ceiling. They stretch as the door closes and contract as it opens. They usually have a safety cable running through the middle.

**Look, don't touch.** Both types are under high tension at all times.

## How torsion springs work

As the door closes, the cables wind the spring tighter around its bar, storing energy. When the door opens, the spring unwinds and turns the drums, which lift the door through the cables. Torsion systems usually give smoother, more balanced movement and put less strain on the opener.

## How extension springs work

Extension springs stretch along the tracks when the door closes. When it opens, they pull back and lift the door through a set of pulleys and cables. They are common on older and lighter doors. If an extension spring breaks without a safety cable inside it, it can whip across the garage, which is why that cable matters.

## Torsion vs. extension at a glance

| | Torsion springs | Extension springs |
|---|---|---|
| Location | Bar above the door | Along both side tracks |
| Movement | Usually smoother and more balanced | Can be less even as the springs age |
| Safety when they break | Spring stays on the bar | Needs safety cables to stay contained |
| Common on | Most modern and heavier doors | Older and lighter doors |

## How long garage door springs last

Springs are rated in **cycles**: one cycle is one opening and one closing. The rating depends on the spring, so ask your technician what is installed. A door used several times a day wears its springs faster than one used once a day. Cold weather, rust and a lack of lubrication also shorten their life.

## Warning signs of a worn spring

- The door feels heavy or will not stay put when stopped halfway
- One side lifts before the other
- A gap appears in a torsion spring's coils
- Loud bangs, squeaks or jerky movement
- The opener strains or reverses

## Why spring replacement is a professional job

Winding bars, set screws and stretched springs hold enough energy to cause serious injury. Specialist tools and training are needed to release and set that tension safely. A technician will also:

- Match the springs to your door's weight and size
- Check the cables, drums, bearings and rollers that work with the springs
- Balance the door and test the opener's safety reverse

Some doors can be converted from extension to torsion springs. Ask about it during your [spring repair](/services/broken-spring-repair/) estimate.
