Garage door springs can break at any time of year, but many homeowners notice that it happens on a freezing morning. Cold does not break a healthy spring by itself. It adds stress to a spring that is already worn, and that is often the moment it finally gives way.

## Why cold weather is hard on springs

### Metal becomes less forgiving

Steel springs flex thousands of times over their life. In very low temperatures the metal is less tolerant of stress, and small fatigue cracks that have been growing for months are more likely to fail.

### Lubrication thickens

Grease on the springs, rollers and hinges stiffens in the cold. The door moves less freely, so the springs and the opener work harder on every cycle.

### Rust and moisture

Snow, salt and slush carried in on cars raise the humidity in a garage. Rust pits the surface of the spring coils and weakens them over time.

### More cycles in winter

Many families use the garage door more in winter because they park inside. Every open and close is one more cycle on springs that have a limited life. Standard torsion springs are commonly rated for around 10,000 cycles, though this varies by spring.

## Warning signs before a spring breaks

- The door feels heavier or the opener strains to lift it
- The door rises unevenly or jerks
- You see a gap or stretched coils on a torsion spring
- Rust on the coils
- A loud bang from the garage, which is often a spring breaking

If a spring has already broken, the door may open only a few inches or not at all. Read [signs of a broken garage door spring and what to do](/blog/broken-garage-door-spring-signs-and-what-to-do/).

## How to lower the risk this winter

- **Book a tune-up before the first hard freeze.** A technician can check spring condition, balance and hardware. See our [maintenance and tune-up service](/services/maintenance-tune-up/).
- **Use a garage-door lubricant** on hinges and rollers, not general-purpose grease that stiffens in the cold.
- **Watch for slow or heavy movement** and get it checked early.
- **Keep the bottom seal in good shape** to limit snow and water getting in. See [garage door weatherstripping](/blog/garage-door-weatherstripping/).

Our [winter garage door maintenance checklist](/blog/winter-garage-door-maintenance-checklist/) covers the full list.

## Never adjust or replace springs yourself

Springs hold enormous tension. Adjusting, unwinding or replacing them without the right tools and training can cause serious injury. If you think a spring is worn or broken, stop using the door and call a technician.
