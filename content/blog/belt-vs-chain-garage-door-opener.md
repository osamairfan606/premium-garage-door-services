Choosing a new garage door opener mostly comes down to three things: how quiet it needs to be, how heavy your door is, and how much ceiling space you have. The three common types are chain-drive, belt-drive and wall-mount (jackshaft) openers. Here is how they compare.

## Chain-drive openers

A metal chain pulls a trolley along a rail to raise and lower the door.

- **Strength:** handles heavy doors well
- **Noise:** the loudest type, with a clanking chain and vibration
- **Maintenance:** the chain needs occasional adjustment and, on many models, lubrication
- **Best for:** detached garages, or anywhere noise is not a concern

## Belt-drive openers

A reinforced rubber belt replaces the chain, on the same rail-and-trolley design.

- **Strength:** suits most residential doors
- **Noise:** much quieter and smoother than chain drives
- **Maintenance:** little to none
- **Best for:** attached garages, especially with a bedroom or living space above or beside the garage

## Wall-mount (jackshaft) openers

The motor mounts on the wall beside the door and turns the torsion spring bar directly. There is no overhead rail.

- **Space:** frees the ceiling for storage, high-lift tracks or a car lift
- **Noise:** very quiet, with less vibration through the house
- **Requirements:** usually needs a torsion spring system and enough side room next to the door
- **Best for:** high or cathedral ceilings, and owners who want a clean, open garage ceiling

## Quick comparison

| | Chain drive | Belt drive | Wall mount |
|---|---|---|---|
| Noise | Loudest | Quiet | Quietest |
| Ceiling space | Needs a rail | Needs a rail | No overhead rail |
| Maintenance | Some | Low | Low |
| Typical fit | Detached garages, heavy doors | Most attached garages | High ceilings, torsion-spring doors |

## Features worth looking for

- **Battery backup**, so the door works during a power cut
- **Smartphone control** and alerts when the door is left open
- **Rolling-code remotes**, so codes cannot be copied easily
- **Soft start and stop**, which reduces wear and noise
- **Safety sensors and auto-reverse**. Read [how safety sensors work](/blog/garage-door-safety-sensors/).

## The door matters as much as the opener

An opener is designed to guide a balanced door, not to lift its full weight. Before installing a new opener, a technician should check the springs, cables and rollers. Putting a new opener on a door with worn springs shortens the opener's life. See [torsion vs. extension springs](/blog/torsion-vs-extension-springs/).
