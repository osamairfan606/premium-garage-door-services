A garage door can fail at the worst moment: late at night, before work, or with the car still inside. The first priority is safety, the second is securing your home. This guide covers what counts as an emergency, what to do right away and what to tell the technician when you call.

## Is it an emergency?

Call for emergency service if:

- The door is **stuck open** and your home or belongings are exposed
- The door is **stuck shut** and you need your vehicle
- A **spring or cable has snapped**
- The door is **off track**, crooked or hanging unevenly
- The door was hit by a vehicle
- The door moves on its own or will not stop

A noisy door, a slow opener or a dead remote is usually not an emergency and can wait for a regular appointment.

## First, make it safe

1. **Keep people, pets and cars away** from the door and its path.
2. **Stop using the opener.** Unplug it if you can do so safely.
3. **Do not try to lift or force the door.** A door with a broken spring is very heavy and can drop suddenly.
4. **Do not touch springs, cables or brackets**, because they are under high tension.
5. **Do not pull the emergency release** if the door is open or off track. The door could fall.

## If the door is stuck open

- Move valuables out of view and lock the door between the garage and the house.
- If the door is level and the springs look intact, a technician may talk you through closing it safely over the phone. Do not try it if anything looks broken or crooked.
- Keep lights on in and around the garage until help arrives.

## If the door is stuck shut with your car inside

- Do not run the opener repeatedly. If the springs have failed, you can burn out the motor or bend the door.
- Arrange other transport if you can, and tell the technician the car is trapped so they understand the urgency.

## If the door is off track or crooked

An off-track door can fall without warning. Keep everyone clear and do not try to push it back into the track. Our [off-track repair](/services/off-track-repair/) page explains what the fix involves, and our article on [why you should stop using a door that is off track](/blog/garage-door-off-track/) has more detail.

## What to tell the technician

Having these details ready saves time:

- What happened: a bang, the door stopping halfway, a hit from a car
- Whether the door is open, closed or stuck partway
- What you can see: a gap in a spring, a hanging cable, rollers out of the track
- The door's size (single or double) and the opener brand, if you know it
- Your address or ZIP code and the best phone number to call back
