Many garage door opener problems can be fixed with a new remote battery, sensor alignment or a replacement part. But an opener does not last forever. Here are the signs that replacing the opener makes more sense than another repair.

## 7 signs it is time for a new opener

### 1. The opener is old

Openers often last 10 to 15 years, depending on use and maintenance. If yours is older than that and starting to fail, a replacement is usually the better investment.

### 2. It has no safety sensors or auto-reverse

Openers sold in the US since the 1990s must have entrapment protection, such as photo-eye sensors near the floor. An opener without them is a safety risk, especially for children and pets. See [how garage door safety sensors work](/blog/garage-door-safety-sensors/).

### 3. Repairs keep coming back

If you are paying for one repair after another, such as gears, logic boards or travel limits, the money is often better spent on a new unit.

### 4. It is very loud

Older chain-drive openers can be noisy, which matters if there is a bedroom above or next to the garage. Belt-drive and wall-mount models are much quieter. Compare them in [belt vs. chain vs. wall-mount openers](/blog/belt-vs-chain-garage-door-opener/).

### 5. It is slow or unreliable

An opener that hesitates, stops part way or only works on the second or third press is wearing out, or the door itself has a problem.

### 6. Old remote security

Very old openers use fixed codes that are easier to copy. Modern openers use rolling codes that change each time you press the remote.

### 7. You want features it cannot offer

Battery backup for power cuts, smartphone control, open-door alerts and keypad entry are standard on many new openers. See [smart garage door openers](/blog/smart-garage-door-openers/).

## Check the door before blaming the opener

An opener is designed to guide a balanced door, not to lift its full weight. If the springs are worn or broken, or the rollers and tracks are binding, the opener strains and fails early. A new opener on an unbalanced door will wear out quickly too. A technician should check the door's springs, cables and balance first.

If the opener has simply stopped, try the checks in [garage door opener not working? 6 things to check](/blog/garage-door-opener-not-working/) before deciding.
