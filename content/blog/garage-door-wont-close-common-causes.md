A garage door that won't close leaves your home and belongings exposed, so it is natural to want a quick fix. The good news is that the cause is often simple. Work through the checks below in order, and stop if anything looks damaged or unsafe.

## 1. Something is blocking the safety sensors

Every modern opener has two small sensors near the bottom of the tracks. If anything breaks the invisible beam between them, the door will reverse or refuse to close. Check for:

- Boxes, bins, bikes or leaves in the doorway
- Cobwebs or dirt on the sensor lenses
- Snow or ice building up at the base of the tracks in winter

Wipe the lenses gently with a soft cloth and try again.

## 2. The sensors are out of alignment

Each sensor has a small light. When the light on either side is off or blinking, the sensors usually cannot "see" each other. A knock from a car bumper or a lawnmower is enough to move them. Loosen the bracket, line the sensors up so both lights are steady, and tighten it again. If they still blink, the wiring may be damaged. Our [garage door repair](/services/garage-door-repair/) visits include sensor diagnosis.

## 3. The close-limit setting needs adjusting

The opener has a limit setting that tells it where "closed" is. If it is set too short or too far, the door may stop early or touch the floor and bounce back up. Check the opener manual for the limit adjustment. If you are unsure, a technician can reset it quickly.

## 4. The remote or wall button has a problem

If the door responds to the wall button but not the remote, replace the remote battery first. If neither works, check that the opener is plugged in and that the lock or vacation mode is not switched on at the wall control.

## 5. The tracks are bent or the rollers are worn

Look along the metal tracks on both sides. Dents, gaps or rollers sitting outside the track can stop the door from closing. **Do not keep running the opener if the door is off track**, because it can come loose and fall. We handle this with [off-track repair](/services/off-track-repair/) and [roller repair](/services/roller-repair/).

## 6. A spring or cable has failed

If the door looks uneven, moves very slowly or the opener strains, a spring or cable may have broken. This is a job for a professional. Read our guide to [broken garage door springs](/blog/broken-garage-door-spring-signs-and-what-to-do/) to learn the warning signs.

## 7. The opener itself is failing

Older openers can lose force, strip gears or develop circuit-board faults. If the motor hums but nothing moves, or the opener is many years old, it may be time to repair or replace it. See our [garage door opener repair and installation](/services/garage-door-opener-repair-installation/) service.

## When to call a professional

Call a technician if:

- The door is crooked, off track or makes grinding or banging noises
- You suspect a broken spring or loose cable
- The sensors are aligned and clean but the door still reverses
- You need the door secured tonight
